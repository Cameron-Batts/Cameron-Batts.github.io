Portfolio Return and Tail Risk Simulation

Compare empirical bootstrap and fitted Student t simulations for a diversified seven-ETF portfolio.

At 95% confidence, the simulated lower-tail mean of terminal wealth from $100 is $68.83 for the bootstrap and $75.61 for the fitted t simulation. These are simulated wealth values, not loss estimates.

Uses a recorded adjusted-price snapshot for 2020–2022 and 5,000 one-year paths. End dates are inclusive, and returns without an observed prior close are dropped. Bootstrap draws assume independent days; fitted t log returns can produce extreme tail values.

Portfolio Volatility and Risk Model Comparison

Compare Gaussian GARCH specifications, one-day VaR exceedances, and approximate capital reserves across forecast horizons.

GJR-GARCH has the lowest training-sample AIC among the three specifications. All three recorded zero 95% one-day VaR exceedances during the 30-day test window, which is too short to establish reliable tail-risk performance.

Uses a 2020–2024 training sample and the first 30 trading days of 2025 as a short test window. Multi-day reserves approximate risk by summing forecast variances. This is not an exact multi-day GARCH loss distribution, and VaR exceedances alone do not validate expected shortfall.

Mortgage Default Survival Analysis

Compare Cox proportional hazards specifications and estimate a hypothetical loan survival curve over 120 months.

The exploratory comparison covers 304,709 loans. Categorical-coefficient warnings make the scenario estimates provisional: estimated survival is 95.26% through month 120 and a 0.0619% probability of default during month 120 under the model.

The PURPOSE and full Cox fits raised possible infinite-coefficient warnings, indicating separation or weakly identified categories. Their effect estimates and scenario predictions remain provisional. Specifications use identical complete observations for AIC comparison. Adjacent equal-predictor intervals are combined while retaining every time gap, predictor change and event boundary; a 1,000-loan comparison verifies the same coefficients and partial likelihood as the monthly representation. Prepayment is treated as censoring. Default-only survival is not a competing-risk cumulative incidence estimate. No held-out calibration or proportional-hazards diagnostics are claimed.

Mortgage Default and Loan-to-Value Sensitivity

Join loan origination and performance histories, fit a default hazard model, and compare a higher loan-to-value scenario.

The hypothetical default probability changes from 3.50% to 4.57% over 61 months when loan-to-value rises by 10 points. The fitted hazard ratio is 1.3127.

The scenario raises original loan-to-value by 10 percentage points with other covariates fixed. This is an in-sample association, not a causal effect. Prepayment is treated as censoring. The workflow estimates default probabilities; it does not complete an amortizing expected-dollar-loss model.
